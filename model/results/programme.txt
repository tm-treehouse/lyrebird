The error instrument reads +1.24 dB against classic SNDR on average, spread +1.16 to +1.37 dB.
The offset is expected and its two causes are known. The classic
figure rescales the surviving noise bins to cover the ones it
excised -- ten harmonic lobes and the signal lobe out of about 850

the two paths are slid 256 samples apart. It is the check that
matters most: the STF of this topology is exactly 1 with zero delay,
so a wrong settling offset would show up here rather than as a
confidently wrong noise figure. A plain lag argmax cannot do this

Q2: IS 3 dB THE RIGHT HEADROOM BUDGET?   [892 s]
==========================================================================
The 3 dB recommendation comes from one probe: a tone at Fs/4 sampled
at 45 degrees, whose reconstructed waveform peaks exactly 3.01 dB
above every one of its samples. The question is what real material
does, so the first table is the peak the cascade actually presents
to the quantizer against the peak the PCM contains. Records are
faded at both ends and scanned in blocks; an un-faded record's edge
reads as 1 dB of headroom that is not there.

'peaks apart' is how far the largest reconstructed peak sits from
the largest sample. For unprocessed material they are the same
event. For clipped material they are not even close, and a headroom
measurement windowed on the loudest sample measures the wrong
moment.

Read the first column with the last but one. The probe is 3.010 dB of
overshoot because every one of its samples sits at full scale by
construction. Unprocessed material never does that, and its
overshoot is correspondingly small. Clipping is what creates flat
runs at full scale, and clipping is what creates the overshoot.

==========================================================================

modulator.max_stable_amplitude and endtoend.max_stable_pcm -- no
