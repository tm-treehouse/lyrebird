==========================================================================
Two findings from this script have now dissolved into measurement
artefacts, so this one is checked before it is believed. The test is
the one that would break it: a longer transform, which halves the bin

THE LOW-FREQUENCY FAULT WAS THE TRANSFORM, NOT THE LOOP   [16373 s]
==========================================================================
README.md carries an open item: the loop occasionally settles into a

x - x.mean() nulls the record's unwindowed average. A Kaiser-windowed

was looking for.**

Two things follow. The open item in README.md -- the low-frequency

this, and there is no limit cycle to fix. And the rule in the trap
list is not strong enough as written: 'remove the mean' has to say
*which* mean, because the difference between the two is invisible in
the time domain and 8.5 dB in the answer.

==========================================================================
The question was posed against the limit cycle README.md leaves
open. That fault turns out not to exist -- it was the mean
subtraction, measured in the section above -- so what is left is the
general version, and it is still the one the hardware has to answer:
if something ever does put energy between 20 and 100 Hz that is not

Each case is run four ways that differ only in things that cannot

expected to trip any more; the point is what the three detectors

Three detectors, all 20-100 Hz power.
  output    : the element sum itself. What a monitor on the output
              sees, and all it sees.
  error     : output against the ideal reconstruction. Correct, and

==========================================================================
MEASUREMENT TRAPS THIS RUN FOUND   [16416 s]
==========================================================================
Each one produced a confident number that was wrong, and each is
demonstrated here rather than asserted.

1. 'REMOVE THE MEAN' HAS TO SAY WHICH MEAN.
   x - x.mean() nulls the record's unwindowed average. A Kaiser-
   windowed transform integrates sum(w*x)/sum(w). The difference is
   invisible in the time domain and it lands as a DC residue smeared
   across twelve bins, which is 280 Hz here -- on top of the 20 to
   100 Hz band. Measured above: 8.5 dB on an in-band figure and
   53 dB on the low band, and it is the whole of the low-frequency
   limit cycle README.md leaves open. Every mean removed in this
   module is the windowed one.

2. THE GAIN FIT HAS TO HAVE THE MEAN REMOVED FROM IT TOO.
   Element mismatch leaves a static offset on the element sum. The
   error has its mean removed, which is the rule README.md already
   states -- but the *scalar gain* fitted before that subtraction
   does not, and at 23.4 Hz bins a Kaiser main lobe is twelve bins
   wide, so the offset leaks to 280 Hz, inside the band the fit is
   taken over. The bias it leaves scales as the signal shrinks.

    PCM level   fitted gain   error, mean left in   error, mean removed
        -12.0      0.998570                 -88.4                -133.9
        -40.0      0.998569                 -88.4                -122.0
        -70.0      0.998556                 -88.4                -124.1

   Left in, the error stops moving when the level does, which is the
   tell: it is no longer measuring the chain. It cost this run one
   complete pass and a finding that turned out not to exist.

3. THE GAIN FIT HAS TO BE RESTRICTED TO THE BAND.
   Fitted over the whole record it is dominated by the modulator's
   out-of-band noise, which is 90 dB above the in-band error. That
   noise is uncorrelated with the signal but its inner product with
   it is not zero, and the leftover gain error injects a pure
   in-band residual. Predicted at about -77 dBFS; measured:

   broadband fit: gain 0.998569, in-band error -124.3 dBFS
   in-band  fit: gain 0.998570, in-band error -135.7 dBFS

4. A HEADROOM WINDOW HAS TO BE CENTRED ON THE RECONSTRUCTED PEAK,
   NOT ON THE LOUDEST SAMPLE. The two are the same event in
   unprocessed material and 1.5 seconds apart in clipped material,
   because clipping makes the reconstruction overshoot wherever a
   flat run happens to be, not where the record is loudest. Same
   record, same bisection, only the window moved:

          centred on the loudest sample: MSA -3.75 dBFS, peak at the quantizer -0.39 dBFS
      centred on the reconstructed peak: MSA -5.39 dBFS, peak at the quantizer -0.51 dBFS

5. A LAG CHECK OVER A FEW SAMPLES CANNOT CHECK ANYTHING.
   The signal is band-limited to 20 kHz and sampled at 24.576 MHz,
   so its autocorrelation is flat over plus or minus eight samples
   and an argmax there reports where the out-of-band noise fell. It
   false-alarmed on 34 of 90 segments of real material. What is
   checked instead is the in-band error at the offset used against
   the in-band error 256 samples away, which is a fifth of a period
   at 10 kHz and therefore a real displacement.

alignment guard: the in-band error at the offset used is at least 27 dB below what it becomes
when the two paths are slid 256 samples apart, over 138 segments that had signal to check.
