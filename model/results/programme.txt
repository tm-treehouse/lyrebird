construction. Unprocessed material never does that, and its
overshoot is correspondingly small. Clipping is what creates flat
runs at full scale, and clipping is what creates the overshoot.

Three things to take from this.

The error is in the 2-20 kHz band, 13 dB above the 100 Hz-2 kHz
band and 24 dB above 20-100 Hz. That is the shape 0008 promises:
rotation turns mismatch into an error the loop pushes up out of the
way, and it is still doing that against real material.

The draw matters by 3.3 dB across five of them, all at the same one percent
specification. A single mismatch seed is one board, not the answer.

Trimming each bank's total to exactly seven is worth -0.0 dB, which is nothing: the
rotation already handles the bank total. Making the two banks
identical is worth +1.4 dB, which says the two sides' errors are
independent and add in power, as they should. Neither is buildable;
both are here to show which property of the elements the number
actually depends on, and the answer is the spread, not the sum.

Q3: CAN GENUINE BASS AND THE LOW-FREQUENCY FAULT BE TOLD APART?   [969 s]
==========================================================================
README.md: a tripped run puts every bit of its excess in the 20 to
100 Hz bins. Sustained bass puts its signal in the same bins. If the
only thing available is how much energy comes out down there, the
two are indistinguishable and the hardware cannot flag one without
flagging the other.

The wander is not a switch. It is a state the loop falls into, and
README.md says a perturbation of 2^-40 decides which. So each case
is run four ways that differ only in things that cannot change the
audio -- how many samples the loop ran before the window opened, and
which way the datapath rounds ties -- and the runs that wander are
identified from the result rather than arranged in advance.

Three detectors, all 20-100 Hz power.
  output    : the element sum itself. What a monitor on the output
              sees, and all it sees.
  error     : output against the ideal reconstruction. Correct, and
              not available in hardware -- it needs a reference DAC.
  loop, v-u : the modulator's output code minus its own input. The
              STF of this topology is exactly 1 with zero delay, so
              this cancels the music exactly and leaves the shaped
              error. It is the d the integrators are already driven
              by, so the FPGA has it for free.

==========================================================================
MEASUREMENT TRAPS THIS RUN FOUND   [1013 s]
==========================================================================
Each one produced a confident number that was wrong, and each is
demonstrated here rather than asserted.

1. THE GAIN FIT HAS TO HAVE THE MEAN REMOVED FROM IT TOO.
   Element mismatch leaves a static offset on the element sum. The
   error has its mean removed, which is the rule README.md already
   states -- but the *scalar gain* fitted before that subtraction
   does not, and at 23.4 Hz bins a Kaiser main lobe is twelve bins
   wide, so the offset leaks to 280 Hz, inside the band the fit is
   taken over. The bias it leaves scales as the signal shrinks.

    PCM level   fitted gain   error, mean left in   error, mean removed
        -12.0      0.998570                 -88.4                -130.3
        -40.0      0.998568                 -88.4                -121.8
        -70.0      0.998518                 -88.4                -123.5

   Left in, the error stops moving when the level does, which is the
   tell: it is no longer measuring the chain. It cost this run one
   complete pass and a finding that turned out not to exist.

2. THE GAIN FIT HAS TO BE RESTRICTED TO THE BAND.
   Fitted over the whole record it is dominated by the modulator's
   out-of-band noise, which is 90 dB above the in-band error. That
   noise is uncorrelated with the signal but its inner product with
   it is not zero, and the leftover gain error injects a pure
   in-band residual. Predicted at about -77 dBFS; measured:

   broadband fit: gain 0.998569, in-band error -124.0 dBFS
   in-band  fit: gain 0.998570, in-band error -133.8 dBFS

3. A HEADROOM WINDOW HAS TO BE CENTRED ON THE RECONSTRUCTED PEAK,
   NOT ON THE LOUDEST SAMPLE. The two are the same event in
   unprocessed material and 1.5 seconds apart in clipped material,
   because clipping makes the reconstruction overshoot wherever a
   flat run happens to be, not where the record is loudest. Same
   record, same bisection, only the window moved:

          centred on the loudest sample: MSA -3.75 dBFS, peak at the quantizer -0.39 dBFS
      centred on the reconstructed peak: MSA -5.39 dBFS, peak at the quantizer -0.51 dBFS

4. A LAG CHECK OVER A FEW SAMPLES CANNOT CHECK ANYTHING.
   The signal is band-limited to 20 kHz and sampled at 24.576 MHz,
   so its autocorrelation is flat over plus or minus eight samples
   and an argmax there reports where the out-of-band noise fell. It
   false-alarmed on 34 of 90 segments of real material. What is
   checked instead is the in-band error at the offset used against
   the in-band error 256 samples away, which is a fifth of a period
   at 10 kHz and therefore a real displacement.

alignment guard: the in-band error at the offset used is at least 26 dB below what it becomes
when the two paths are slid 256 samples apart, over 138 segments that had signal to check.
