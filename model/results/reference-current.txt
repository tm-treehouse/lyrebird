==============================================================================
THE REFERENCE RAIL: TRANSITION RATE, NOT CODE
==============================================================================

0008 guarantees exactly seven elements high at every code, so the DC
load on the reference is constant. It guarantees nothing about how
often lines CHANGE, and the 180 pF filter capacitors and the
registers both draw current in proportion to that. The reference
sets full scale, so it multiplies the signal: a signal-correlated
current on it is distortion, not noise.

READ THIS AS A PREDICTION, NOT A MEASUREMENT. The model has no
supply in it. What is measured here is a digital proxy -- how many
of the element lines change state each clock -- converted through
hardware constants taken from hardware/lyrebird-dac-reva/. Every
step of that conversion is stated so it can be rechecked or
rescaled. The thing that would settle it is a bench measurement of
the rail with the modulator running.

H1. The proxy, and a check on it before anything is believed.

    lines high per sample, one channel : min 7, max 7  (0008's invariant)
    rising == falling per sample       : True
    with rotation, T == 2*min(k+k', 14-k-k') : True
    without rotation, T == 2*|k-k'|          : True

    Those two identities are exact, and they are the whole story.
    With rotation the pointer advances BY THE CODE, so consecutive
    windows are disjoint until they wrap, and the number of lines
    that change is k + k' folded about seven. Writing k = 3.5 + s
    with s the signal in code units, that is

        T  =  14 - 4|s|

    a full-wave rectifier. The transition rate carries the
    magnitude of the signal, so its spectrum is the even harmonics.
    Predicted mean for a -3.7 dBFS tone: 14 - 4*(2A/pi) = 8.1789
    Measured mean                              : 8.1555
    Predicted second harmonic amplitude        : 3.8808 transitions
    Measured                                   : 3.8343 transitions

    Closed form and simulation agree to under two percent, from
    two independent directions. The proxy is doing what it claims.

H2. Null tests, so a large number is not read off an untested rule.

    digital silence   : T mean 13.2708  component at 2 kHz 1.026e-03 transitions
    DC at -20 dBFS    : T mean 12.5805  component at 2 kHz 4.050e-04 transitions

    Level scaling: the model says the second harmonic is
    proportional to amplitude, so halving the tone should halve it.
    tone at   -3.7 dBFS: T mean  8.1555  h2  3.8343  closed form  3.8808
    tone at   -9.7 dBFS: T mean 11.0353  h2  1.8533  closed form  1.9450
    tone at  -15.7 dBFS: T mean 12.4407  h2  0.8028  closed form  0.9748
    tone at  -23.7 dBFS: T mean 13.1493  h2  0.1833  closed form  0.3881

H3. The transition rate against the element-selection rule.
    'SNDR 1%' is the ordinary in-band figure with one percent
    elements, which is what the rotation exists to protect, put
    beside the transition rate it costs.

                                        rule   T mean         h2         h4   SNDR 1%
     plain DWA, pointer advances by the code    8.155  3.834e+00  7.306e-01    133.79
                                 no rotation    1.188  1.931e-02  2.880e-02     57.05
           pointer advances by 1 each sample    4.020  5.241e-02  3.661e-02     84.23
           pointer advances by 3 each sample    7.987  3.512e+00  4.501e-01     84.82
                  pointer stepped by an LFSR    5.546  1.494e+00  1.091e-03     77.82
                   both sides on one pointer    7.505  3.632e+00  6.202e-01     81.33

    The two columns move together and in the wrong direction.
    Everything that lowers the transition rate's signal content
    also breaks the mismatch shaping, because they are the same
    mechanism: DWA shapes mismatch to first order precisely
    BECAUSE the pointer advance equals the code, and that is what
    ties the number of changing lines to the code. A fixed step
    takes 37 dB off the transition rate's second harmonic and 50 dB
    off the mismatch figure. This is not a pointer rule that has
    been chosen badly; it is a property of the method.

H4. Converting to a rail current and to an output figure.

    reference rail            3.32 V
    element, two halves       1.668k + 1.668k
    filter capacitor          180 pF per element, tau = 150 ns against a 40.7 ns clock
    register Cpd              30 pF per flip-flop (SCES021L)
    rail source impedance     10 mOhm in band
    lines on the rail         28 = two channels of 14

    The capacitor term is simulated rather than counted: at 150 ns
    into a 40.7 ns clock the capacitor moves 24 percent of the way
    per sample and never reaches either rail, so charge per
    transition is well under C*V. Counting transitions and
    multiplying by C*V overstates it about threefold.

    Both channels are given the same signal, which is the worst
    case and the one mono content produces.

                                        rule   elements  registers   AC at 2f        out  broadband
     plain DWA, pointer advances by the code    20.08mA    19.96mA   11.103mA    -101.10     -136.8
                                 no rotation    14.96mA     2.91mA    0.070mA    -120.98     -124.5
           pointer advances by 1 each sample    19.75mA     9.84mA    1.654mA    -115.02     -135.2
           pointer advances by 3 each sample    19.95mA    19.55mA   10.316mA    -101.14     -135.9
                  pointer stepped by an LFSR    19.43mA    13.58mA    5.143mA    -105.90     -129.5
                   both sides on one pointer    19.93mA    18.37mA   10.580mA    -101.10     -133.5

    'out' is the in-band power of x*g, where x is the element sum
    and g the fractional gain error the rail ripple produces, with
    the lobe at the signal frequency taken out: a rail ripple at 2f
    against a signal at f puts half its product back on the
    fundamental, which is a gain change and not audible as
    distortion, and half on the third harmonic, which is. 'broadband'
    is what is left with every harmonic lobe excised too.
    Both compare directly against the -136.5 dBFS in-band noise of
    the chain as built, which is the 132.8 dB figure at a -3.7 dBFS signal.

    Where the plain-DWA error sits, by harmonic:
      harmonic 2 at   1992.2 Hz :  -161.43 dBFS
      harmonic 3 at   2988.3 Hz :  -101.14 dBFS
      harmonic 4 at   3984.4 Hz :  -159.68 dBFS
      harmonic 5 at   4980.5 Hz :  -121.22 dBFS
      harmonic 6 at   5976.6 Hz :  -160.72 dBFS
      harmonic 7 at   6972.7 Hz :  -131.46 dBFS

H6. Real material.

                  material   T mean    T std   in-band g        out
            programme-like   11.472    2.027       -94.8    -104.41
                percussive   13.082    1.225      -126.7    -138.33
      sustained 41 Hz bass   10.749    2.286       -92.6     -98.95

    Real material does not escape it. The transition rate follows
    the envelope, so the rail ripple is the programme's own
    low-frequency envelope and the products land under it.

H5. Which term, so the answer can be rescaled.

                      term on the rail      mean      at 2f        out
                       capacitors only   20.08mA    1.718mA    -115.55
                        registers only   19.96mA    9.385mA    -102.91
                                  both   40.04mA   11.103mA    -101.10

    The register term is the larger of the two and it rests on
    reading the datasheet's 30 pF Cpd as per flip-flop, which
    power.md calls its largest remaining uncertainty. If it is per
    package instead, the register row drops by 24 dB and the
    capacitors-only row is the answer. Both rows are above the
    chain's own floor, so the uncertainty changes the size of the
    problem and not whether there is one.

H7. What it comes to.

    Plain DWA, mono, one percent elements:
      transition-dependent current  +6.2 mA of capacitor charging and 20.0 mA of register
      current at the second harmonic 11.10 mA, which on 10 mOhm is 111 uV on 3.32 V
      equivalent in-band output error -101.1 dBFS, of which broadband -136.8 dBFS
      against the chain's own         -136.5 dBFS
      and the 110 dB target, which is -113.7 dBFS at this level

    What would have to change. The rail ripple is at twice the
    signal frequency and at the programme envelope, which is to say
    it is an AUDIO-frequency current. Local decoupling does not act
    there: eight 100 nF parts are 100 ohm at 2 kHz and the
    regulator's own 10 mOhm is what the current sees. 0008's
    suggested mitigation, heavy local decoupling at the register
    packages, is right for the megahertz content and does nothing
    for this.
    To put this term at the chain's own floor would take 35 dB
    less rail impedance in band, i.e. 169.73 uOhm at 2 kHz, which no
    regulator does and which would need farads of bulk capacitance.

    What follows from the identity instead. T = 14 - 4|s| is bounded
    above by 14, and the modulation is the deficit. Toggling unused
    or dummy lines to make up the deficit -- a transition ballast of
    up to 14 toggles per clock, driven by the same code -- would
    flatten the current without touching the selection rule or the
    mismatch shaping. That costs the average current of a fully
    toggling array, which is the opposite of a power saving, and it
    is a hardware change rather than a model result. It is offered
    as the one mitigation the measurement actually points at.

H8. Cross-checks, because the result is large.

    An independent arithmetic route to the same number: a rail
    ripple of amplitude G at 2f against a signal of amplitude A at f
    puts A*G/2 on the third harmonic. Measured against that, and
    swept over things the identity says should and should not
    matter.

                          case   T mean    I at 2f         H3      A*G/2    diff
           48 kHz, 1 kHz, -3.7    8.155   11.103mA    -101.14     -99.23   -1.91
          192 kHz, 1 kHz, -3.7    8.155   11.103mA    -101.14     -99.23   -1.91
         44.1 kHz, 1 kHz, -3.7    8.155   10.364mA    -101.71     -99.83   -1.88
          48 kHz, 200 Hz, -3.7    8.155   11.103mA    -101.14     -99.23   -1.91
           48 kHz, 5 kHz, -3.7    8.155   11.103mA    -101.14     -99.23   -1.91
            48 kHz, 1 kHz, -10   11.133    4.773mA    -116.35    -112.87   -3.48
            48 kHz, 1 kHz, -20   12.936    0.974mA    -137.85    -136.68   -1.18

    Sample rate and tone frequency change nothing, which is what
    T = 14 - 4|s| requires: the mean and the second-harmonic
    amplitude of a rectified sine depend on its amplitude alone.
    The 44.1 kHz row differs because its element clock does, which
    is the check that the pipeline is responding at all. Level
    does change it, and faster than the signal: the ripple is
    proportional to amplitude and the product to amplitude
    squared, so the third harmonic sits 97 dB below a -3.7 dBFS
    signal and 118 dB below a -20 dBFS one. This is a large-signal
    mechanism, worst exactly where the 110 dB target is specified.
    Simulation runs 1 to 3 dB below the closed form throughout,
    because the loop's own shaped noise fills in the rectifier's
    corner that a pure sine would leave sharp.
