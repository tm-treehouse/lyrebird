========================================================================
SIGNAL WORD: TAPERED VERSUS FLAT, MEASURED ON THE WHOLE CHAIN
========================================================================
A half-band stage has two phases. The filtered phase rounds because
it accumulates products. The pass-through phase is the centre tap
against an interpolation gain of two -- a wire -- and it rounds only
if the next word is narrower than the one it already sits on. A
tapering chain therefore re-rounds every sample at every stage, for
nothing: the storage it saves is under one percent.

========================================================================
ACCUMULATOR, PER STAGE  (flat 26-bit signal word)
========================================================================
Each product is rounded into the accumulator, which is the
pessimistic reading: a MAC whose output register is the accumulator
rounds every product, not only the final sum. The criterion is that
the accumulator must not be the louder of the two noise sources in
its own stage -- at or below 0.5 dB added to the signal word's.

The L1 norm times the largest peak the cascade can present, sqrt(2),
is the hard bound on the accumulator: 4.06 at the three steep stages,
under 2 from stage 6 on. The signal word needs one bit above the sign
-- the inter-sample peak is 1.414 and anything past that is already
overloading the quantizer and belongs clipped, not represented.

========================================================================
WHICH WAY TIES GO
========================================================================
Round-half-up is one adder and is what you reach for first. It is
also biased: a word already on a finer grid ties half the time and
every tie goes up. The offset that leaves is far below any LSB in
the chain, and it is not harmless -- a delta-sigma loop turns a
static input offset into idle tones in the lowest bins of the band.

Worst case is 96 kHz: an offset of -169.2 dBFS costs -0.0 dB of SNDR.
The broadband rounding noise is the same either way, so convergent
rounding buys this for logic alone. Note that half-up does not fail
at every rate: it leaves the same offset everywhere and the loop
only sometimes lands on a bad pattern. That makes it a latent fault,
not a visible one.

========================================================================
LOW-FREQUENCY LIMIT CYCLES  (measured, not fixed)
========================================================================
The loop occasionally settles into a very-low-frequency wander that
lands at the bottom of the audio band. When it does, every bit of
the excess sits in the 20-100 Hz bins and the in-band figure comes
back 15 to 19 dB low. It is a property of the loop, not of the
datapath: adding 2**-40 at the modulator input switches it on or
off, while adding 1e-18, which float64 discards, does not.

6 input variations per rate, at the recommended widths. The
second row of each pair adds 0.25 LSB of dither at the quantizer,
inside the loop, which is the textbook remedy for idle tones.

========================================================================
RECOMMENDATION
========================================================================
Signal word: 26 fractional bits, 1 integer bit, 1 sign = 28 bits, flat across all nine stages. Flat, not tapered:
a narrowing word re-rounds the pass-through phase at every stage.
Accumulator: 2 integer bits and 1 sign above the point, and below it
   stages 1-3: 31 fractional bits, 4 above the point, 35 bits total
   stages 4-5: 30 fractional bits, 3 above the point, 33 bits total
   stages 6-9: 29 fractional bits, 2 above the point, 31 bits total
Round ties to even everywhere: half-up leaves an offset the modulator
turns into low-frequency idle tones.
One thing is measured and not fixed: see the limit-cycle section.
